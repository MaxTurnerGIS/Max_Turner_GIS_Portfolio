SHEET SC·23 · REMOTE SENSING / CHANGE DETECTION

Storm-Driven Coastal Change, Santa Cruz, CA

Mapping how the January 2023 atmospheric river storms redrew the Santa Cruz coastline, pixel by pixel.

In January 2023, a sequence of atmospheric river storms battered the California coast, causing widespread flooding and erosion. Using Sentinel-2 Surface Reflectance imagery in Google Earth Engine, I built a before/after change-detection pipeline across the greater Santa Cruz coastline. The workflow used cloud-masked median composites for November to December 2022 and January to February 2023, a Modified Normalized Difference Water Index (MNDWI) to separate land from water, and a thresholded three-class change map: stable land, stable water, and land converted to water.

To validate the map, I ran a stratified random accuracy assessment (120 reference points) against high-resolution imagery, producing a confusion matrix and bias-corrected, area-adjusted estimates. Final cartography was laid out in ArcGIS Pro.

SHEET NFL·GIS · APPLIED PROGRAMMING

Home Field Advantage Analyzer

A desktop GUI that turns 58 seasons of NFL box scores into a spatial win-percentage map.

Built entirely in Python, this tool loads a season-by-season dataset of 13,464 NFL games (1966 to 2024, 44 franchises) and lets a user pick a team and year range. It filters the data with pandas, groups games by stadium coordinates (merging renamed or relocated venues into a single site), and renders a color-coded matplotlib map of win percentage by location, embedded live inside a custom tkinter GUI.

Example: filtering for the Seattle Seahawks (1975 to 2020) surfaces a 368-game record of 228-140. The split is clear: a roughly 62% win rate at their home turf (CenturyLink Field / Husky Stadium / Seattle Kingdome), against a single road loss logged at University of Phoenix Stadium.

SHEET JMU·25 · FIELD SURVEY / ECOLOGICAL GIS

Tracking Amphibian Activity, Edith J. Carrier Arboretum

Two weeks of GNSS-logged fieldwork on where amphibians choose their cover, and why.

Amphibians are picky: they need nearby water, shelter from predators and sun, and stable humidity and temperature. Over two weeks in spring 2025, I surveyed two sites at JMU's Edith J. Carrier Arboretum: the Arboretum Pond and a stratified 50ft × 20ft sampling grid at the forest/playground edge. Every observation was logged with a Trimble Juno 3B GNSS receiver, building on a 2017 to 2018 baseline herpetological survey of the same site.

The count flipped the baseline survey's pattern almost exactly: where the 2017–18 study logged mostly salamanders, I found 10 American Bullfrogs clustered at the pond's shallow, vegetated edges and just 1 Eastern Red-backed Salamander, tucked under a downed log. All GNSS points were digitized and cartographically finished in ArcGIS Pro.
